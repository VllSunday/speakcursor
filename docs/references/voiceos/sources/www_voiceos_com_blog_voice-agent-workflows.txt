VoiceOS maps each example to its official integration catalog, so you can see the action, the platform, the confirmation behavior, and what to check afterward.
Written byAndrew Hoffmann
PublishedSeptember 30, 2026
Forty documented actions, with a check for each
VoiceOS’s integration catalog is the starting point for these examples. Every action below is matched to a tool definition in the shipped catalog, including its platform boundary and whether it asks first. That is source verification, not forty completed live tests.[1]
The screenshots show VoiceOS in everyday use: drafting a Slack reply, preparing a note or reminder, working on a Notion request, finding videos, and drafting a Linear issue. Each image opens at full size. Preview and in-progress states are described where they appear.
Use disposable notes, events, files, and issues for an evaluation. Connect only the accounts you mean to use. The sample requests describe an intent; their success still depends on permissions, required fields, account state, and the product version.

How to turn a capability into evidence
For each action you actually test, record the app version, operating system, integration, starting state, request, preview, result, and any manual repair. Inspect the destination instead of relying only on the assistant’s completion message. Keep failures in the record.
A task is complete when the intended result is present in the right app and account.
A tool marked “No confirmation listed” may run without a separate approval step. Read operations and local writes are not equivalent, even if both have that label. Check the requested operation before invoking it, and keep your first tests limited to disposable material.
If you later publish a tested workflow, include a permissioned recording or transcript and a clear description of what succeeded. Until that evidence exists, describe it as a documented capability or proposed test. This distinction makes the catalog useful to a reader deciding what to try.

Frequently asked questions
Were all forty actions tested live for this article?
No. Each action was checked against the shipped integration catalog. The verification steps are a test plan, not a record of completed end-to-end trials.
Do all actions ask before running?
No. The table preserves the catalog’s per-tool confirmation behavior. Some local writes and read operations do not list a separate confirmation.[1]
Why do some sections say Mac only?
Native Apple integrations in the catalog have a Mac-specific platform requirement. A VoiceOS Windows installation does not add those Apple-only integrations.[1]
