What is a wake word?
Definition
A wake word is a chosen phrase, such as "Hey Siri" or "Alexa," that a voice system listens for continuously in order to know when to start processing what you say. Detection runs as a small, dedicated model, typically on the device itself, and only after the wake word is recognized does the system begin handling the actual request.
Why wake words exist
An assistant that processed everything it heard would be unacceptable for both privacy and accuracy. The wake word acts as a spoken push-to-talk: a low-power local detector watches for one phrase and gates everything else behind it, which is how always-available assistants avoid being always-recording services.
Wake words on the desktop
Phones and smart speakers made wake words familiar, but desktop voice tools often prefer a hotkey instead: at a keyboard, a key press is faster and unambiguous. Hands-free desktop modes may use a wake word, open listening with visible state, or voice activity detection, depending on the product.
Common failure modes
False accepts (the assistant wakes to a similar-sounding phrase or the TV) and false rejects (it misses you in noise) are the classic trade-off pair. Vendors tune sensitivity between them, which is why wake words are multi-syllable and phonetically distinctive.
Frequently asked questions
Is a wake-word device recording everything?
The wake-word detector runs continuously by design, but mainstream implementations process that audio locally in a short rolling buffer and transmit or store audio only after the wake word triggers. Product documentation and OS indicators are the place to verify specifics.
Do I need a wake word on a computer?
Usually not: a hotkey is faster at a keyboard, which is why desktop tools like VoiceOS center on press-to-talk with hands-free modes for when your hands are unavailable.
